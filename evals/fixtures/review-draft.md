# Review of "Pre-treatment resting-state EEG alpha power and ECT response in treatment-resistant depression"

**Recommendation:** Major Revision

## Summary

The authors examined whether pre-treatment EEG alpha power predicts ECT response in a retrospective cohort. This is a reasonable question and the dataset is of interest. My concerns are three: missing data reporting, calibration, and the strength of the conclusions.

## Comments to the authors

**1. Missing data.**

The manuscript does not state how missing data were handled. This is essential for a retrospective cohort with incomplete covariates, and the absence makes the results difficult to interpret. The authors should report how much data were missing for each variable, and how the missing values were handled.

**2. Sample size.**

The authors state that the study enrolled 186 patients. This figure should be reconciled with the flow diagram and the per-protocol sample.

**3. Calibration.**

No assessment of calibration is reported anywhere in the manuscript. Discrimination alone is insufficient to characterise a prediction model; a model with acceptable discrimination may be badly miscalibrated. The authors should add a calibration analysis.

**4. Strength of conclusions.**

The overall analysis is invalid and the conclusions cannot be trusted. The authors should substantially weaken their claims.

**5. Citation accuracy.**

The authors cite Okafor et al. (2018) in support of their calibration approach. This reference should be checked: the year does not match what I would expect for this work.

## Minor points

- The abstract is appropriately cautious in its conclusions.
- Figure 1 is clear.
